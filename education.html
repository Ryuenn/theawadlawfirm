FREQUENTLY ASKED QUESTIONS

Georgia Personal Injury
Questions Answered

Straight answers from an Atlanta personal injury attorney — not a chatbot.

Under O.C.G.A. § 9-3-33, you have 2 years from the date of injury to file a personal injury lawsuit in Georgia. Missing this statute of limitations will permanently bar your claim — the court will dismiss it regardless of how strong your evidence is. Narrow exceptions exist for minors and for injuries not immediately discoverable, but these are rare. Do not wait. Contact an attorney immediately to preserve your rights and your evidence.

Georgia applies modified comparative fault under O.C.G.A. § 51-12-33. If you are less than 50% responsible for the accident, you can still recover damages — but your award is reduced proportionally by your share of fault. At exactly 50% or more, you recover nothing. Insurance adjusters aggressively push fault onto accident victims specifically to use this rule against them. Having an attorney prevents this from destroying your recovery.

You are not legally required to hire an attorney, but settling without one is rarely in your best interest. Insurance adjusters are professionally trained to minimize payouts — they do this full-time. Research consistently shows that represented accident victims receive significantly higher net compensation than those who negotiate alone. At Awad Law Firm, we work on contingency — no fees unless we win — so there is no financial risk to getting expert representation from the start.

Georgia allows recovery for economic damages — medical expenses, lost wages, future care costs, and property damage — and non-economic damages including pain and suffering, emotional distress, loss of consortium, and loss of enjoyment of life. Punitive damages are available in egregious cases such as DUI collisions; Georgia caps punitive damages at $250,000 in most personal injury cases under O.C.G.A. § 51-12-5.1, with exceptions for DUI and intentional acts.

Move to safety, call 911, photograph the scene thoroughly, collect witness contact information, and seek prompt medical treatment even if you feel fine. Notify your own insurer of the accident. Decline a recorded statement to the other driver's insurance company — you are not legally required to give one and it will be used against you. Avoid social media posts about the accident or your injuries. Then contact a Georgia personal injury attorney as soon as possible.

There are no upfront fees, no hourly charges, and no litigation costs to you unless and until we recover compensation on your behalf. Your attorney's fee is a percentage of the final settlement or court verdict. This arrangement makes high-quality legal representation accessible to every Georgian — regardless of financial circumstances.
